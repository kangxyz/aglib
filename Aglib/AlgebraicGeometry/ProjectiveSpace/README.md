# Polynomial projective space

This family specializes Mathlib's actual `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj` to the
total-degree grading of `MvPolynomial ι R`. A coordinate labeling
`ι ≃ Fin (n + 1)` gives projective `n`-space; `ULift (Fin (n + 1))` supplies
coordinates in an arbitrary base-ring universe. The construction permits
every commutative base ring and every coordinate type in that universe;
finiteness of the coordinate type is stated where needed.

`Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.ProjectiveSpace.Basic` uses the lower
[`MvPolynomial` result](../../RingTheory/MvPolynomial/GradedMap.lean)
identifying the irrelevant ideal with the ideal generated by the coordinate variables. Its
`coordinateAffineOpenCover` is the native affine cover with rings
`HomogeneousLocalization.Away` and native `Proj.awayι` maps. The
`coordinateOpen` family covers the whole scheme, and every nonempty finite
intersection is affine. `coordinateProductSectionsIso` is exactly Mathlib's
canonical homogeneous-localization-to-sections isomorphism.

`Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.ProjectiveSpace.Structure` identifies the actual
degree-zero component with `R` and constructs `toSpec : ProjectiveSpace R ι →
Spec R` from the native `Proj.toSpecZero`. For finite `ι` the morphism is
proper. If `R` is also Noetherian, the projective space is locally Noetherian.
`coordinateChart_toSpec` proves that the chart-to-base morphism is induced by
the actual inclusion of coefficients into the homogeneous localization.

`Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.ProjectiveSpace.Constants` extends this equation
to every positive homogeneous basic open. Its `constant_restriction`
identifies the actual restriction of a global structural constant, defined
using `toSpec.appTop` and `ΓSpecIso.inv`, with its original homogeneous
constant fraction under the canonical section map. This supplies the
base-ring normalization used by the Laurent section comparison.

[GlobalFunctions](GlobalFunctions.md) identifies the original structural
coefficient map `R →+* Γ(ProjectiveSpace R I, ⊤)` as a bijection for a finite
nonempty coordinate type `I`. `globalFunctionsRingEquiv` has exactly that
forward map, normalized by `toSpec.appTop` and `ΓSpecIso.inv`. This holds over
any commutative ring, including the zero ring, with no point of the projective
scheme chosen.

`Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.ProjectiveSpace.CoordinateRing` proves the full
chart calculation over every commutative ring. `coordinateRingEquivPolynomial`
identifies the native degree-zero localization at `Xᵢ` with the polynomial
ring on the remaining variables. Its forward map is dehomogenization
`Xᵢ = 1`; its inverse sends `Xⱼ` to the actual fraction `Xⱼ / Xᵢ`.
`polynomialToCoordinateRing_dehomogenize` proves the numerator/denominator
formula for every homogeneous polynomial. `coordinateOpenIsoSpecPolynomial`
transports this proved equivalence to the actual coordinate-open scheme.

[FieldCoordinates](FieldCoordinates.md) factors an actual map
`Spec K ⟶ ProjectiveSpace R I` through an original standard chart, recovering
that same morphism. `chartHomCoordinates` evaluates its original coordinate
ratios and gives one in the selected coordinate. The relative factorization
retains the original coefficient map `R → K`. The coordinate type is arbitrary;
the categorical factorization uses the native common universe for `R`, `K`
and the index, while coordinate evaluation allows an independently sized
commutative target ring.

`Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.ProjectiveSpace.Transition` constructs the units
`Xᵢ / Xⱼ = Xᵢ² / (Xᵢ Xⱼ)` on the native two-coordinate localizations. The
`coordinateOverlapToTriple` maps are the native homogeneous-localization
maps. The units satisfy the cocycle law on triple overlaps, as do their
integer powers. `coordinateTransitionSectionUnit` transports them by the
canonical section maps; its cocycle theorems use the actual structure-sheaf
restriction maps. This supplies explicit gluing data for every integer
twist over arbitrary commutative rings and in arbitrary dimension.

`Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.ProjectiveSpace.Twisting` uses the proved generic
[rank-one gluing construction](../Modules/Invertible/Gluing.lean) to construct
the actual module sheaf `twistingSheaf R ι n = O(n)` for every integer `n`; every twist is
locally of rank one (`isLocallyRankOne_twistingSheaf`).
Its local coordinates satisfy `sᵢ = (Xᵢ / Xⱼ)^(-n) sⱼ`, corresponding to
the standard frames `Xᵢ^n`. `twistingLocalIso` and `twistingSectionsEquiv`
are the canonical chart projections, and the transition theorem retains
the actual original units. `Twisting.Frames` records the original generator
as the inverse image of `1`, with exact restriction, coefficient
reconstruction and generator transition formulas. The same standard-cover singleton bases prove
finite local freeness, finite presentation and quasicoherence.

`Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.ProjectiveSpace.HomogeneousSections` constructs an
actual global section of `O(n)` from every degree-`n` homogeneous polynomial,
for `n ≥ 0`. Its local coefficient is the canonical fraction `p / Xᵢ^n`.
The transition identity is proved in the original homogeneous localization
and transported through the original structure-sheaf maps. In particular,
the global section `Xᵢ^n` has local coefficient `1` in its own chart.

`Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.ProjectiveSpace.Multiplication` multiplies actual
twist sections by multiplying their original matching local functions.
The map is bilinear over the original section ring and respects every
restriction. `twistingMultiplyGlobal` is an actual module-sheaf morphism
`O(b) → O(a+b)` for a global section of `O(a)`; the polynomial specialization
is multiplication by the original chart fraction `p / Xᵢ^n`.
`ProjectiveSpace.Tensor` descends this original bilinear product through the
native sheafification adjunction to `O(a) ⊗ O(b) → O(a+b)`. Its pure-tensor
formula and its standard-frame coefficient formula are literal products.
`Tensor.Isomorphism` glues the original tensor generators to an explicit
inverse and proves both inverse equations. Thus `twistingTensorIso` identifies
the native tensor with `O(a+b)` for all integers over arbitrary commutative
rings, with the original multiplication as its forward map.
[Tensor/Monoidal](Tensor/Monoidal/README.md) makes the twisting sheaves a braided
monoidal functor `Discrete ℤ ⥤ (ProjectiveSpace R ι).Modules` with these
isomorphisms as structure maps, writes multiplication by global sections as
whiskering, and proves the comparisons of composite twist multiplications by
monoidal coherence.
`Tensor.Coherent` retains the original finite-coproduct comparison and each
summand's multiplication map. Applying exact tensor to the proved two-step
presentation proves that twisting preserves actual coherent modules over
Noetherian bases.

`Tensor.Flat` proves that twisting preserves relative flatness over any base
(`twistingTensor_relativeFlat`; `𝒪(d)` is trivial on every standard chart), and that every
twisting sheaf and every sum of twisting sheaves is relatively flat over `Spec R`
(`twistingSheaf_relativeFlat`, `twistingCoproduct_relativeFlat`), from `flat_toSpec` and
`Scheme.Modules.relativeFlat_unit_of_flat` (`Modules/Flat/Relative/Unit`).
`Tensor.Equivalence.Hom` identifies morphisms `𝒪(-d) ⟶ M` with the global sections of
`𝒪(d) ⊗ M`, linearly over any structural ring map (`negativeTwistHomLinearEquivSections`),
through `twistingTensorNegIso : 𝒪(d) ⊗ 𝒪(-d) ≅ 𝒪` and
`Scheme.Modules.unitHomGlobalSectionsLinearEquivOfHom`. It is a different map from
`twistingGlobalSectionsHomLinearEquivOfHom` of `Hom.Linear`, which identifies morphisms between
twists with sections by multiplication; it is not a duplicate.

[Flat](Flat.lean): `toSpec R ι` is flat (`flat_toSpec`, an instance) for every ring and
coordinate type. On each standard chart it is the spectrum of the chart coefficient map, which
is flat (`flat_coordinateRingCoefficient`), being the inclusion of `R` into a polynomial ring.
Coefficient base change along a flat ring map is flat by `BaseChange/Flat` (`flat_map`); the
two leaves are independent.

`Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.ProjectiveSpace.GlobalGeneration` proves that the
original global sections `Xᵢ^n` define an epimorphism from the free structure
module on `ι` onto `O(n)` for every `n ≥ 0`. Its evaluation on each original
free section is recorded, and local surjectivity follows from the coefficient
`1` in its chart. `coordinatePowerGeneratingSections` is the native globally
generating section family; it is finite when `ι` is finite. This is global
generation of the nonnegative twists themselves. `TwistPresentation` below
supplies finite negative-twist presentations for arbitrary affine-locally
finite-type coefficients.

[Monomials](Monomials.lean): `Monomials ι r`, the exponents of degree `r`, and their numbering
`monomialsEquiv ι n e r : Monomials ι r ≃ ULift (Fin ((n + r).choose r))` for `e : ι ≃ Fin (n + 1)`.
[HomogeneousEvaluation](HomogeneousEvaluation.lean): `homogeneousEvaluation ι r s : 𝒪^I ⟶ 𝒪(r)` for
homogeneous polynomials `s : I → A[x]_r` (`_freeSection`, `_coordinatePower`), the monomial basis
`monomialFamily := homogeneousPieceBasis`, `monomialSection` and the monomial map
`monomialMap ι A r := homogeneousEvaluation ι r (monomialFamily ι r)`, with `ιFree_monomialMap`.
Its base change is [BaseChange/MonomialMap](BaseChange/MonomialMap.lean).

[FromSections](FromSections.md) constructs `FromSections.mapOfFiniteFamily`
from a structural map `p : X ⟶ Spec R`, an actual locally rank-one module `M`
and a finite family of section morphisms whose coproduct map is epi. The result
is an actual map `X ⟶ ProjectiveSpace R I`, with the original structural triangle
and exact coordinate-open inverse images. `twistingPullbackIsoOfFiniteFamily`
identifies the pullback of the actual `O(1)` with `M`; every pulled native
coordinate map followed by this isomorphism is its originally prescribed
section map. No flatness, Noetherianity or quasi-compactness is required.

[Source naturality](FromSections/Naturality.md) retains the original coordinate
index and constructs the pulled section family under every morphism `g : T ⟶ X`.
Its generation follows from the original epimorphism, and
`mapOfFiniteFamily_pullback` gives equality of the composite projective map
with the map of that actual pulled family. The isomorphic-module version
retains the specified section-preserving comparison. Each generating family
has its own `O(1)` comparison from `FromSections` above.

[FromSections/LineSource](FromSections/LineSource.md) starts with locally
rank-one modules `D` and `J` and an original finite epimorphic row `d i : D ⟶ J`.
Its canonical section family belongs to the actual coefficient line module
`Dᵛ ⊗ J`, using the existing dual-evaluation and dual-tensor equivalence.
`LineSource.mapOfFamily` constructs the projective map over the original base,
and `twistingPullbackIso` identifies its actual `O(1)` pullback with that
coefficient module. `pulledCoordinate_triangle` retains each original map
under canonical duality, and `recoveredCoordinate_eq` recovers literal equality
with `d i`. No frames or alternative spanning family are chosen.

[FromSections/FieldSpan](FromSections/FieldSpan.md) treats a commutative ring
`A`, field `κ` and ring map `β : A →+* κ` in the native common universe. For a
finite original family `a : I → κ`, the full span
`Submodule.span A (Set.range a) = ⊤`, using exactly `Module.compHom κ β`, makes
its literal original `mapOfFiniteFamily` a closed immersion. Generation and
finiteness of `β` follow from that same span; no index or section map is replaced.
`mapOfFiniteFamily_eq_chart_of_ratios` identifies the original projective map
with a given chart map from its coefficient square and actual normalized-ratio
equations at a nonzero original coordinate. `mapOfFiniteFamily_eq_chart_of_values` derives that coordinate to be one. These chart comparisons retain the finite index needed
by the constructor, with no full-span or finite-ring-map premise.

[AwayAlgebra](AwayAlgebra.lean) makes every degree-zero localization
`HomogeneousLocalization.Away (homogeneousSubmodule ι R) f` an `R`-algebra (`awayAlgebra`: the
algebra map is the coefficient map, the scalar action is Mathlib's), with `awayMapAlgHom`,
`coordinateRing_algHom_ext` and the overlap restrictions `overlapLeft`, `overlapRight`. Its scalar
action agrees with Mathlib's `HomogeneousLocalization.instSMul` only at instances transparency:
`rw`/`simp` with `zero_smul`, `Algebra.smul_def` or `add_smul` fail on `•` goals over these rings,
while term-mode applications work (rewrite with `smul_eq_algebraMap_mul`). Over `ℤ` it forms the standard
`ℤ`-algebra diamond with `Ring.toIntAlgebra`. [SchemeOver](SchemeOver.lean) gives
`schemeOver R ι`, projective space as an object of `Over (Spec R)`.

The functor of points of projective space, in `Grassmannian.localFunctor` form, comes from the
comparison with the Grassmannian of rank-one quotients described in the
[Grassmannian README](../Grassmannian/README.md). This family keeps the twisting sheaves: the chart
quotients of the universal point are the frame coefficients of `coordinatePowerGeneratingMap R ι 1`.

`Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.ProjectiveSpace.PrincipalOpen` proves that the
actual nonvanishing open of `Xⱼ/Xᵢ` is exactly the original chart
intersection. `SectionExtension` applies native affine localization to
actual quasicoherent module sections on these opens. It constructs local
numerators with one common exponent on a finite coordinate cover and
retains the original ratio/transition equations. `SectionExtension.Overlap`
kills all overlap discrepancies with one further coordinate power.
`SectionExtension.Global` then constructs an actual map `O(-N) → M` from
every section on a standard chart, using the original internal-Hom sheaf.
Its formula on every subopen of that chart is multiplication by the original
restricted section in the standard frame; in particular the local generator
maps to that section with coefficient `1`. Only quasicoherence and finite
coordinates are required. Finite twist presentations use this extension
for finite local generating families. `SectionExtension.Bounds` composes
with multiplication by `Xᵢ^(N-N₀)` to extend the same generator from every
`O(-N)` with `N ≥ N₀`; the selected-chart coefficient remains exactly `1`.

`SectionExtension.Colimit` proves the other half of the chart colimit
([Stacks 01PW](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01PW), Hartshorne II.5.14). Suppose a
morphism `O(-r) → M` vanishes on the generator of the selected chart. Then its precomposition
with `Xᵢ^(N-r)` is zero for all large `N` (`exists_negativeTwistCoordinatePowerMap_comp_eq_zero`;
`twistingGenerator_eq_zero_iff_exists_comp_eq_zero` is the iff form, for some `N`). The proof
applies the chart annihilation on every chart and uses one exponent for all charts. On another
chart `j`, the coordinate-power map multiplies the frame coefficient by `(Xᵢ/Xⱼ)^(N-r)`. These
maps compose (`negativeTwistCoordinatePowerMap_comp`, `_self`), so the groups `Hom(O(-r), M)`
form a directed system, linear over any structural ring map.
`negativeTwistHomDirectLimitLinearEquiv` identifies its `Module.DirectLimit` with
`Γ(D₊(Xᵢ), M)` by chart evaluation. Only quasicoherence and finite coordinates are required. The
transport to the direct limit of `Γ(P, M(r))` under multiplication by `Xᵢ`, through
`negativeTwistHomLinearEquivSections`, is not formalized.

`TwistPresentation.Sections` proves that every affine-locally finite-type
module on finite-coordinate projective space admits finitely many actual
maps `O(-N) → M` with one common `N` above any prescribed bound, whose
native coproduct map is surjective on the section
module of every standard chart. The finite index is the union of chosen
finite original chart generator sets. The result works over arbitrary
commutative rings. `TwistPresentation.Epimorphism` proves that the resulting
actual finite coproduct map is an epimorphism, by native affine-section
detection and open-cover detection of its cokernel. Its theorem retains
surjectivity on every original chart section module as well.
`TwistPresentation.Coherent` adds the Noetherian base hypothesis and proves
that the actual kernel is coherent. It supplies the original native short
exact kernel sequence, ready for cohomological induction. Neither the
epimorphism nor its coherent-kernel proof assumes cohomological finiteness
or Serre vanishing. Applying the construction to that actual kernel also
gives a two-step exact presentation by finite sums of negative twists,
with an epimorphic final quotient map. Each stage can use a single common
negative degree, and both degrees can be required to exceed any prescribed
bound. The original varying-degree interfaces delegate to these constructions
at bound zero.

These interfaces supply the standard charts for the projective twist and
Cech computation in [Stacks 30.8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01XS).
The [projective cohomology family](../Cohomology/Projective/README.md) now
computes finite free actual twist cohomology and its sharp vanishing bound,
and proves actual coherent cohomological finiteness on polynomial projective
space over every Noetherian base ring. Its closed-immersion consumer also
proves embedded projective finiteness with the original structural map to
the affine base. The actual tensor isomorphism and coherent tensor preservation
also give Serre vanishing with one bound for all positive original cohomology
and all larger twists. The
[projective-space duality family](../Cohomology/Duality/Projective/README.md)
supplies the original trace/Yoneda perfect pairing for all complementary
degrees of coherent sheaves over an arbitrary field.

The lower dependencies are Mathlib's projective spectrum, homogeneous
polynomials, variable ideal and finite lattice operations. The immediate
consumers are the projective structure morphism, chart algebra and the
implemented projective cohomology family. No existing theorem-family input is
discharged by the chart results alone.

`Hom.Coefficients` and `Hom.Sections` identify actual morphisms
`O(b) → O(a+b)` with original global sections of `O(a)`. The inverse section
is the explicit matching family of the morphism's values on original local
generators; its restriction and transition formulas are proved before the
two inverse equations. The forward map is exactly `twistingMultiplyGlobal`.
`Hom.Linear` proves the same equivalence is linear for every specified
`α : A →+* Γ(P,top)`, using native scalar restriction on sections and the
native category-center action on morphisms. Geometry and structural-scalar
universes may differ. No finite-coordinate, field or Noetherian premise is used.

[Tensor/Unit](Tensor/Unit.lean) identifies the actual zero twist with the structure module: the forward map is the original local frame coefficient, and the inverse is multiplication by the original globally glued coefficient-one section. [Tensor/Equivalence](Tensor/Equivalence.lean) uses the native sheafified two-line associator, original multiplication, and unit maps to construct tensor equivalence by every integer twist. Its forward and inverse functors are literally tensor by `O(d)` and `O(-d)`; native adjointification supplies the triangle constraint. These results require only a commutative base ring and the common scheme/coordinate universe, with no finite-coordinate hypothesis.

[Tensor/Equivalence/Sections](Tensor/Equivalence/Sections.lean) records the
original composition and unit maps on pure sections, including their twist
product and scalar formulas.

[AffineImmersion](AffineImmersion.lean) embeds the spectrum of a finite-type
ring map into an actual standard chart of polynomial projective space.
The chart map is a closed immersion, its composite with the original
chart inclusion is an immersion, and the structural factorization is
literal. The existential endpoints require neither a chosen algebra
instance nor a Noetherian or field hypothesis. This is a geometric
prerequisite for the graph-closure route to Chow modifications.

[AffineImmersion.Open](AffineImmersion/Open.lean) transports this construction
through the original affine-open `isoSpec`. Every affine open of a locally
finite-type scheme over `Spec R` therefore has an actual immersion into finite
polynomial projective space with exactly the original restricted structural
map. No Noetherian or properness hypothesis is needed for this transport.

[BaseChange](BaseChange/README.md) proves that actual polynomial projective
space commutes with every coefficient-ring base change. The comparison
uses the original `Proj.map`, standard charts and structural maps, and its
forward map is exactly the native pullback lift of those original maps.
No finite-coordinate, flatness, Noetherian or nonempty-coordinate hypothesis
is imposed. Relative projective projections use this result on affine
base opens. `BaseChange/Twisting` proves `π^* O_R(d) ≅ O_S(d)` with its chart frames,
and the twisted-module form `π^*(O_R(d) ⊗ M) ≅ O_S(d) ⊗ π^* M`; `BaseChange/Flat`
proves that `π` is flat for a flat coefficient map.

[Products.BaseChange](Products/README.md) retains the original base and
component projections when the actual fibre product of a finite projective
product is identified with the native product of its component pullbacks.
It delegates to the sole finite-product comparison and native pullback
symmetry, including the literal empty-family first projection. Finite
coordinates give properness of the original projection and local
Noetherianity over a locally Noetherian new base.

The [derived Serre comparison](../Cohomology/Duality/Projective/Derived/README.md)

## Segre embeddings and finite projective products

[PulledCoordinates](PulledCoordinates.lean) evaluates the original projective
coordinate ratios on arbitrary source subopens. [ChartGluing](ChartGluing.lean)
glues actual chart maps from compatible normalized ratios and detects a closed
immersion from affine chart surjectivity and the exact coordinate-open
preimages. These are the coordinate interfaces used by the Segre construction.

[Segre/ProductChart](Segre/ProductChart.lean) identifies the original binary
projective product charts with the native tensor spectra, retaining both
projections. The [section coordinates](Segre/Sections.lean),
[chart ring map](Segre/ChartRing.lean), and
[section comparison](Segre/ChartSections.lean) give literal products of the
factor ratios and original structural coefficients.
[ChartEvaluation](Segre/ChartEvaluation.lean),
[ChartConstants](Segre/ChartConstants.lean), and
[ChartSurjectivity](Segre/ChartSurjectivity.lean) identify this original section
map with the surjective tensor-chart map.
[Segre/Map](Segre/Map.lean) retains its exact coordinate-open preimages and
structural map. [Segre/ClosedImmersion](Segre/ClosedImmersion.lean) proves that
`Segre.embedding R ι κ` from the actual pullback of the two projective spaces
to `ProjectiveSpace R (ι × κ)` is a closed immersion. The binary statement
holds over every commutative ring and arbitrary coordinate types in the same
universe, without a finite-coordinate or nonzero-ring premise.

[Segre/FiniteProduct](Segre/FiniteProduct.lean) constructs a closed embedding
of a finite native over-product when every factor coordinate type is finite
and nonempty. Its target coordinate type is finite and nonempty, and the
original structure morphism is preserved. The empty product is the original
base spectrum; [Point](Point.lean) embeds it using projective zero-space,
whose original `toSpec` is an isomorphism over every commutative ring.

[FiniteProductEmbedding](FiniteProductEmbedding.lean) applies this embedding
and the existing finite-map embedding theorem to an actual finite map into
such a product. It obtains a closed embedding of the unchanged source into
`ProjectiveSpace R (ULift (Fin (m + 1)))`, with the original structural map.
The finite map itself need not be a closed immersion. The
[projective Chow modification](../Morphisms/Proper/Chow/Projective.lean) is the
current geometric consumer, feeding
[proper coherent Grothendieck existence](../Cohomology/GrothendieckExistence/Proper.md).

## Binary-form multiplication

[BinaryFormProduct](BinaryFormProduct/README.md) constructs the actual coefficient
morphism `(P¹_R)^n → ProjectiveSpace R (ULift (Fin (n + 1)))`. It is finite and
surjective over every commutative ring and for every `n`, including zero; the
coordinate formulas use the coefficients of `∏ i, (x i * T + y i * U)`. Its
original coefficient-map square is cartesian for every homomorphism of
commutative rings `σ : R →+* S`, with the source and target base-change maps
retained. No flatness, reducedness, characteristic or factorial-invertibility
hypothesis is required.

## Quasi-projective schemes over `Spec R`

[QuasiProjective](QuasiProjective.lean) defines `IsHQuasiProjective f` and `IsHProjective f` for
`f : X ⟶ Spec R`: a quasi-compact immersion (closed immersion) into `ProjectiveSpace R ι`, `ι`
finite, over `Spec R` (Stacks 01VW, 01W8 for an affine base; Nitsure's strongly (quasi-)projective,
p. 23, with trivial bundle). Over a Noetherian ring every immersion into `P(ι)_R` is quasi-compact
(`quasiCompact_of_isImmersion_of_isLocallyNoetherian`, Stacks 01OX), so
`isHQuasiProjective_iff_exists_isImmersion`. Comparisons: H-projective implies H-quasi-projective
and proper; H-quasi-projective implies separated, quasi-compact, locally of finite type;
`isHProjective_iff_isHQuasiProjective_and_isProper` (Stacks 0BCL). Closure: projective space,
quasi-compact immersions (`isHQuasiProjective_comp`), closed immersions (`isHProjective_comp`),
any immersion over a Noetherian ring (`IsHQuasiProjective.comp_of_isImmersion`); Noetherian source
(`IsHQuasiProjective.isNoetherian`). [Segre/QuasiProjective](Segre/QuasiProjective.lean):
fibre products by the Segre embedding (`ProjectiveSpace.Segre.productEmbedding`,
`isHQuasiProjective_pullback_fst_comp`, `isHProjective_pullback_fst_comp`, `…snd_comp`).
[AffineImmersion/QuasiProjective](AffineImmersion/QuasiProjective.lean): `Spec A ⟶ Spec R` of
finite type is H-quasi-projective (`isHQuasiProjective_spec_map_of_finiteType`).

[QuasiProjective/Coproduct](QuasiProjective/Coproduct.lean): finite disjoint unions of
H-quasi-projective (H-projective) schemes over `Spec R` are H-quasi-projective (H-projective)
(`isHQuasiProjective_sigmaDesc`, `isHProjective_sigmaDesc`; binary `isHQuasiProjective_coprodDesc`,
`isHProjective_coprodDesc`; colimit cofans and finite disjoint open covers, `….of_isColimit`,
`….of_iSup_eq_top_of_pairwise_disjoint`), for a finite index type in any universe and every
commutative ring (Stacks 0B43 (4), 0B46 (4), over a base with an ample invertible sheaf). The proof
shows that `P(ι)_R ⨿ P(κ)_R` is H-projective (`ProjectiveSpace.isHProjective_coprodDesc_toSpec`):
the linear embeddings into `P(Option (ι ⊕ κ))_R` give a finite morphism from `P(ι)_R ⨿ P(κ)_R`, so
`FiniteEmbedding.exists_closedImmersion_of_isFinite` gives a closed immersion of `P(ι)_R ⨿ P(κ)_R`
into some `P^n_R` over `Spec R`; no disjointness argument is used. Open: base change of the base
ring.

[QuasiProjective/EquivFin](QuasiProjective/EquivFin.lean): an H-quasi-projective `f : X ⟶ Spec R`
has a quasi-compact immersion into `P(κ)_R` over `Spec R` with `κ ≃ Fin (n + 1)`
(`IsHQuasiProjective.exists_isImmersion_equivFin`): the given immersion into `P(ι)_R` followed by
the linear embedding `P(ι)_R ⟶ P(Option ι)_R`, also for empty `ι`; every commutative ring `R` (the
form of Stacks 01VW, with `n + 1` coordinates). Consumer:
[HilbertScheme/Quotient/Existence](../HilbertScheme/Quotient/Existence.lean), for the Hilbert
schemes of an immersed scheme, which take `e : κ ≃ Fin (n + 1)`.

## Projectivity Zariski locally over a base

[ZariskiLocalClosedImmersion](ZariskiLocalClosedImmersion.lean): for `f : X ⟶ S`,
`ProjectiveSpace.ZariskiLocalClosedImmersion f` is the data of open immersions
`j a : Spec A_a ⟶ S` covering `S`, indexed by a type in the universe of `S` (no finiteness), and
closed immersions `c a : X ×_S Spec A_a ⟶ P(ι a)_{A_a}` over `Spec A_a` with
`ι a ≃ Fin (N a + 1)` (Kleiman, arXiv:math/0504020v1, Thm 4.8, p. 27, "projective Zariski locally
over `S`"). `IsZariskiLocallyHProjective f` is the property that every point of `S` lies in an
affine open `Spec R ⟶ S` over which `f` is `IsHProjective`; this is Stacks' "locally projective"
(Definition 29.44.1, Tag 01W8) by Lemmas 29.44.4 and 29.44.8 (Tags 01WB, 01WF). The data give the
property (`ZariskiLocalClosedImmersion.isZariskiLocallyHProjective`), and the property gives data
(`ZariskiLocalClosedImmersion.nonempty_of_isZariskiLocallyHProjective`, through the linear
embedding `P(ι)_R ⊆ P(Option ι)_R`). Further: the open cover `openCover`; for `S` locally
Noetherian the `A_a` are Noetherian (`isNoetherianRing`); flatness and geometric integrality of `f`
pass to the embedded families `c a ≫ toSpec` (`flat_c_comp_toSpec`,
`geometricallyIntegral_c_comp_toSpec`), which are proper (`isProper_c_comp_toSpec`), so `f` is
proper (`isProper`; Stacks, Lemma 29.44.5, Tag 01WC). Consumer:
[Picard/Representability/LocallyNoetherianBase](../Picard/Representability/LocallyNoetherianBase.lean).

[FiniteCoverBaseChange](FiniteCoverBaseChange.lean) base-changes a map to the native projective line along a given map of commutative rings. Its Over arrow is the original pullback map followed by the inverse of BinaryFormProduct.lineOverBaseChangeIso. The same underlying map has its original structure triangle and is finite when the supplied cover is finite. The residue-fibre cover specializes this constructor to the original residue map and chosen pullback.
