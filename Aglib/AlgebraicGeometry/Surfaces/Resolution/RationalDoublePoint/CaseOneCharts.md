# Actual ordinary-blowup chart and child-stalk comparisons

Source: [CaseOneCharts.lean](CaseOneCharts.lean).

This leaf consumes the existing `CaseOne.Presentation` on its literal
saturated chart. `CaseOne.Chart A t` is definitionally the native
`affineBlowupAlgebra A (maximalIdeal A) t`; no new coordinate ring, coefficient
field section, or unsaturated hypersurface model is introduced.

For an actual `BlowupDatum` over `Spec A`, `chart` is its original affine-atlas
chart. `point` is the image of the original prime constructed from the plane
maximal ideal. `stalkIso` is the existing open-chart stalk witness composed
with the native spectrum-stalk isomorphism. `stalkIso_stalkScalar` proves that
this comparison takes the existing original `Hom.stalkScalar` to the exact
`CaseOne.Presentation.localMap`. The point lies over the original closed point.

The original extended maximal ideal is generated by the unchanged original
exceptional parameter. Its minimality in a singular child, the child stalk's
dimension two, and the original plane equation's square membership are
transported from the existing Case-I algebra along that same stalk isomorphism.
`residue_comparison` retains the value on every original residue coefficient;
the Case-I theorem proves bijectivity of the original structural residue map,
not merely existence of an abstract field isomorphism.

Conversely, a globally closed original blowup point lying in this chart and
over the original closed base point has a maximal chart prime containing the
exceptional ideal. Original quotient and ideal map/comap laws construct its
plane maximal ideal and prove that the presentation's prime is precisely the
same original prime. `exists_plane_point` therefore recovers the original
scheme point. Global closedness is an explicit hypothesis here; being maximal
in an affine open is not falsely used to infer global closedness.

The presentation itself must come from the existing full-kernel producer.
This adapter does not construct the exceptional canonical identification,
Gorenstein preservation, the Case-II cubic/marker, or finite termination.
It assumes neither child completeness nor a finite-module structure over the
initial regular base. The independent original singular-point and rational-
propagation theorems supply the appropriate geometric child hypotheses.

The chart and scalar comparisons start over a commutative local ring `A`
with a supplied `CaseOne.Presentation A t g Q` and a plane maximal ideal
containing `Q`. The dimension and singular-parameter results additionally
use a Noetherian domain and the stated nonvanishing of `t` and `Q`.
Residue bijectivity also requires `Q.totalDegree ≤ 2` and exclusion of
every nonzero scalar multiple of an affine-linear square.
The converse point-recovery theorem explicitly requires global closedness,
the original closed-base image, and membership in the chosen chart.
