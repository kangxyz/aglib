# Cartier regularity of the original normal-surface canonical module

The public results are `normalSurfaceCanonical_stalk_regular` and
`normalSurfaceCanonical_cartierSection_mono`. The first states that every
nonzero element of an original source stalk is regular on the same stalk
of `normalSurfaceCanonicalModule`. The second states that the original
`cartierTensorSection` is monic on this canonical module for an actual
closed immersion whose actual ideal module is locally of rank one.

The surface data are exactly those of the existing normal-surface
concentration theorem: an integral source with normal stalks and dimension
at most two, its closed projective embedding over a regular local domain,
dominance, and finiteness of the original generic-image field map. The
projective index is equipped with its actual finite-dimensional indexing
equivalence. There is no additional canonical-module regularity, freeness,
Gorensteinness, or coefficient-frame premise.

At a point of the original polynomial quotient chart, write `S→B` for the
actual surjective local stalk map. The canonical degree is `q`, with
`q + dim B = dim S` supplied by the existing original support and dimension
theorem. For a lift `a:S` of a prescribed nonzero element of `B`, the private
algebra argument derives vanishing of
`Ext_S^q(QuotSMulTop a B,S)` when the element is a nonunit. A zero-dimensional
normal local domain is a field, so that case is impossible. In dimension
one the actual principal quotient has finite length. In dimension two the
prescribed equation extends to an actual regular maximal-primary pair;
the quotient has a regular parameter with finite-length quotient. The
installed regular-sequence Ext concentration proves the required vanishing.
Units act invertibly.

The scalar-Ext regularity theorem applies to the original scalar
short exact sequence. The resulting regularity is transported through the
original ambient-`S`-linear quotient-stalk Ext equivalence and the genuine
ambient top-differential coefficient frame. The canonical coefficient
itself is never framed. A lift through `S→B` identifies its scalar action
with that of the prescribed original local element. The module-valued
open-stalk isomorphism and original derived open homology exchange then
recover regularity on the OWN canonical homology stalk, with the original
coefficient-unit normalization retained.

For the global monicity statement, the original Cartier evaluation and
coefficient cancellation maps show that a section killed by the tensor
section is killed by every section of the actual ideal. The actual local
regular ideal generators have nonzero germs on the integral source.
Canonical-stalk regularity therefore kills each germ of the kernel section;
the sheaf condition gives monicity. This last argument introduces no
quasicoherence or local-freeness requirement on the canonical coefficient.
