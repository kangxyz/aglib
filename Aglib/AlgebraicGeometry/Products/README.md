# Products over the integer base

[IntegerBase](IntegerBase.lean), imported as `Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.Products.IntegerBase`, uses `Scheme.Products.integerBase`, the actual spectrum `Spec (.of (ULift ℤ))` of the universe-lifted integer ring, and the natural map `toIntegerBase` from every scheme. The filename refers to the integer base, not an assumption that the scheme is integral.

`baseProductFunctor` and `baseProductTransformation` use the original fibre products over that base. A compatible morphism `f` with the actual structural-map equation gives `baseProductTransformation_isPullback`. No field or integrality hypothesis is required.

`qcBaseProductFunctor` and its transformation restrict to actual [quasi-compact schemes](../Properties/QuasiCompactCategory.lean), retaining the stated quasi-compactness of the base maps. Relative polynomial/Laurent families are consumers of these canonical products and projections.

[IntegerBaseInterchange](IntegerBaseInterchange.lean), imported as `Aglib.AlgebraicGeometry.Products.IntegerBaseInterchange`, constructs `Scheme.Products.baseProductSwapMap` and `baseProductSwapIso`. For maps `p : S ⟶ integerBase` and `q : T ⟶ integerBase`, the natural isomorphism interchanges the iterated products `(X × S) × T` and `(X × T) × S` over the integer base. Its forward component is the displayed two-pullback lift, and its inverse is the same map with `p,q` exchanged. `baseProductSwapMap_comp` and `baseProductSwapIso_trans` express involutivity; `baseProductSwapMap_naturality` holds for every morphism of the varying scheme. The `baseProductSwapMap_fst_fst`, `baseProductSwapMap_fst_snd` and `baseProductSwapMap_snd` equations fix the original three projections; `baseProductSwapIso_projection_first` and `baseProductSwapIso_projection_second` state compatibility with the product projections. The `baseProductSwapIso_transformation_first` and `baseProductSwapIso_transformation_second` equations retain the supplied structural-map compatibility of a morphism in the corresponding factor.

The ordinary interchange requires no quasi-compactness hypothesis. `qcBaseProductSwapIso` gives the corresponding isomorphism of the quasi-compact-scheme functors under `QuasiCompact p` and `QuasiCompact q`, with its original underlying component and projection/transformation equations. The [relative polynomial–Laurent family](../AffineSpace/PolynomialLaurent/README.md) specializes these comparisons to its actual plus/minus maps.
