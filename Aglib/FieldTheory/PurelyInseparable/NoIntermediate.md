# Algebraic extensions without strict intermediate fields

For an original algebraic field extension F to K, assume every native intermediate
field is bottom or top. The native separable closure first gives either a
separable extension or a purely inseparable one. In the nonseparable case,
a nonbase element of minimal positive exponent supplies an actual exponent-one
simple subextension of degree p. It is not bottom, hence is top, proving that
the original extension has prime degree equal to its positive characteristic.

No Galois, normality, or characteristic-zero hypothesis is imposed. The fields
and their original algebra map are unchanged, and independent universes are
retained. This is a field-theoretic input, not a resolution theorem.
