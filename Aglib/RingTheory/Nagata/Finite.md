# Finite Japanese and Nagata ascent

Import `Aglib.RingTheory.Nagata.Finite`.

All rings and fields lie in one ambient universe u. The Japanese and
Nagata predicates use auxiliary test universe zero, as in
[the coefficient bridges](Basic.md).

`IsFractionRing.finite_of_finite_algebra` starts with a finite algebra
R -> S of domains and supplied fraction fields K and L. The original
maps R -> K -> L and R -> S -> L have explicit scalar towers. They imply
injectivity of the original R -> S map. The conclusion is the actual
`FiniteDimensional K L`.

The proof starts with a finite R-module generating set of S. Native
`IsFractionRing.ideal_span_singleton_map_subset`, applied to the ideal
generated by 1, shows that the same elements span L over K. Algebraicity
needed by that supplier follows from the original finite algebra. Thus
the fraction-field degree is derived, not added as a finiteness premise.

`IsJapaneseRing.of_finite` proves Japanese ascent for a finite injective
map of domains R -> S. Given an original finite extension L of
`FractionRing S`, it constructs the compatible fraction action from
`FractionRing R` and proves that L is finite over it. The intermediate
`FractionRing S` retains the canonical localization action inherited from
R -> S. The Japanese
property of R gives finite `integralClosure R L`.
[`integralClosure.equivOfIntegral`](../IntegralClosure/FiniteOverNormal.md)
identifies this with `integralClosure S L` by the identity on elements
of L. Restricting the finite generators upward to S proves the desired
Japanese condition. S need not be normal, and L need not be separable.

`IsNagataRing.of_finite` permits an arbitrary finite algebra R -> S;
neither ring is required to be a domain. For every prime q of S, let p
be its actual contraction to R. The canonical quotient map

```text
R ⧸ p → S ⧸ q
```

is injective. Its finite module structure is derived from the quotient
of the original finite R-module S and the actual scalar tower through
`R ⧸ p`. Japanese ascent applies to these domains. Noetherianity of S
is proved from the original Noetherianity of R and finite generation.
Together these facts give the Nagata condition at every actual prime.

`IsNagataRing.quotient` specializes finite ascent to the original
quotient algebra by an arbitrary ideal, including the top ideal.

This is the finite-algebra part of
[Stacks 10.162.5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/032T), with the
finite-domain argument from
[10.161.5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BI1).
It does not prove quasi-finite, localization, or arbitrary finite-type
Nagata stability. In particular the analytic-completion and monogenic
arguments needed for surface-chart stability remain separate.
