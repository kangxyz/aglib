# Exact quadratics at original rational double points

Source: [QuadraticDoublePoint.lean](QuadraticDoublePoint.lean).

The native evaluation map has kernel generated by the original shifted
variables `X i - C (alpha i)`. Partial derivation takes the square of an
arbitrary ideal into that ideal. For a polynomial of total degree at most
two in the square of the evaluation kernel, these facts give its exact
centered quadratic with unchanged quadratic coefficients. The Taylor
identity is a polynomial identity over the original field and uses no
division by two, separability, perfection, or coefficient-field section.

The companion principal-ideal result says that nonzero homogeneous
polynomials of the same degree, with one in the principal ideal of the
other, differ by a nonzero scalar. It identifies a genuine nonzero quadratic
relation with an already supplied full homogeneous quadratic kernel. It
does not prove a Hilbert function or manufacture that full-kernel premise.

The immediate consumer is the original Case-I saturated-chart successor.
Its original plane point supplies the evaluation kernel; its exceptional
reduction lifts the resulting centered quadratic to an actual relation in
the original chart. No ambient hypersurface model, new cone, or replacement
residue map is introduced here. The existing `QuadraticClosedPoint` owns
the nonrational-point theorem and original six-coefficient expansion; this
leaf reuses both rather than duplicating them.

Homogeneous binary quadratics retain their literal three original
coefficients. Their native univariate dehomogenization is injective in
degree two. If a nonzero such quadratic has a rational double zero, its
dehomogenization is divisible by the square of the original linear point
polynomial. The degree bound forces a nonzero scalar quotient, and
injectivity homogenizes the equality back over that same field. No
division by two, algebraic closure or square root is used.

A bijective original coefficient ring homomorphism preserves and reflects
the existence of a nonzero scalar homogeneous-square expression. The proof
uses native polynomial-map surjectivity, injectivity and homogeneous
reflection for that exact homomorphism, not a replacement field isomorphism
or a coefficient-field section.

Native degree-at-most-one substitutions retain the degree bound on a
homogeneous linear polynomial. Consequently, a genuine nonsquare centered
plane equation retains a nonzero nonsquare binary part. A nonzero nonsquare
first-coordinate-zero restriction excludes a scalar homogeneous square of
the original ternary polynomial.

Finally, the native evaluation killing the first coordinate maps every
power of the original rational plane-point ideal into the corresponding
univariate point-ideal power. This is the algebraic input for excluding
the opposite blowup charts; the actual geometric chart and overlap maps
remain downstream obligations.
