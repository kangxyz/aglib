# The full kernel of a monic quadratic graded presentation

Let `k` be a field and `H` the original grading of a `k`-algebra `G`.
The source has its native total-degree grading. Its original variables are
`V = X 0`, `S = X 1`, and `T = X 2`.

`relation q₁ q₀` is the literal polynomial

`V² + q₁(S,T)V + q₀(S,T)`.

The binary coefficients are included by `rename Fin.succ`. Their original
coordinate formulas are retained by `finSuccEquiv_binaryInclusion` and
`finSuccEquiv_relation`. If `q₁` is homogeneous of degree one and `q₀` of
degree two, `relation_isHomogeneous` gives the native total-degree-two
statement.

`kernel_eq_span` takes the original graded surjection `π`, this actual
relation in its kernel, and the genuine Hilbert-function equalities
`finrank k (H n) = 2n+1`. It proves that the **entire kernel** is the ideal
generated by the displayed relation. It does not take a presentation,
kernel equality, graded normal form or quotient basis as input.

The proof regroups the original variable V using `finSuccEquiv` and applies
native monic polynomial division. This constructs a representative with
V-degree at most one and proves that its difference from the original
polynomial lies in the actual relation ideal. The same division theorem
proves uniqueness of such representatives.

A homogeneous input has a homogeneous normal representative. To prove
this, take the required homogeneous component of the normal representative.
It still has V-degree at most one. The relation ideal is homogeneous, so
this component represents the same original class. Uniqueness of the monic
normal form identifies it with the full representative.

The degree-n normal subspace is supported on the two actual monomial
families

- `S^(n-i) T^i`, with `i : Fin (n+1)`;
- `V S^(n-1-i) T^i`, with `i : Fin n`.

A constructed equivalence of exponent sets gives dimension `(n+1)+n`.
This includes degree zero, where the second family is empty. The original
graded map is surjective on these normal components: take a homogeneous
component of an arbitrary preimage, then its monic normal representative.
This also derives finite-dimensionality of each target component. Equality
with the supplied Hilbert function makes every normal-component map
injective. Applying this to the homogeneous components of a normal
representative of a kernel element proves that the representative is zero.
Thus the element lies in the original relation ideal.

`quotientEquiv` identifies the literal quotient by that ideal with `G`.
`quotientEquiv_mk` says it sends the class of every original polynomial p
to `π p`; the coefficient and variable corollaries retain the actual
coefficient map and all three original variable images.

Both the actual relation and the genuine Hilbert function in every
nonnegative degree remain explicit inputs for an RDP application. Rationality, Gorensteinness, ordinary-blowup normality and the
relevant Hilbert-function theorem must be supplied independently before
using this leaf in such an application. No coefficient-field section,
completion, perfection, separability, reducedness or algebraic-closure
assumption is used here. The same-field Case-II square argument is separate;
this module makes no RDP, persistent-marking or resolution claim.
