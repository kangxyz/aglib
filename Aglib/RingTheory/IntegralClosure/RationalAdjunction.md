# Rational adjunction over the original normal domain

This module supplies algebraic inputs to the dimension-two rational-adjunction
normalization theorem in `Nagata/RationalAdjunction.lean`. Its rings are the
literal singleton-generated subalgebras in the supplied ambient field.

`Polynomial.ker_aeval_eq_linearRelationIdeal` proves that evaluation at a
fraction over a normal domain has kernel generated by the actual linear
relations. The leading coefficient times a root is integral by the native
integral-normalization polynomial; normality puts that element in the original
coefficient ring. Subtracting the resulting linear relation lowers degree.

`Algebra.adjoinSingletonQuotientEquiv` identifies the quotient by the actual
generator with the quotient by `Algebra.numeratorIdeal`. For a supplied
equation `a*x=b`, with `a` nonzero, `numeratorIdeal_eq_colon` identifies this
ideal with `(b):(a)`. Every comparison is an algebra equivalence over the
original coefficient ring. `Ideal.quotientColonSingletonHom` is the explicit
injective linear map induced by multiplication by `a`, into the principal
quotient by `b`. It is a linear map, not a ring map.

The dimension lemma uses a nonzero annihilating polynomial and the native
dimension bound for a surjection killing a nonzerodivisor. No catenarity,
Japanese stability or normalization-finiteness premise enters these results.

`Algebra.nonempty_adjoinSingletonAtPrimeEquiv` constructs the actual localization
comparison when the coefficient localization is a DVR and the generator
belongs to the selected prime. An inverse to the fraction in the coefficient
localization would put a denominator outside the prime into the numerator
ideal. DVR total divisibility therefore places the fraction itself in the
coefficient maximal ideal. Both directions of the resulting localization
equivalence are checked through the original supplied fraction field. The
constructed equivalence is returned existentially, so its private proof
helpers remain in proof context under Lean's module visibility rules.
`isDiscreteValuationRing_atPrime_adjoin_singleton` obtains its coefficient
DVR input from the associated-prime colon injection and transfers it across
that equivalence. No dimension hypothesis is needed for this local comparison.

`adjoinSingletonAwayEquiv` compares the principal open obtained by inverting
the original denominator with the original coefficient localization. It
preserves the supplied field inclusion and transfers integral closedness.
`polynomialValueInclusion` and its finite-module theorem construct the actual
inclusion `R[f(x)]` into `R[x]` for a positive-degree monic polynomial.

The residue-polynomial supplier uses the actual contracted prime, its
fraction field and the original maximal quotient. It returns a polynomial
whose value lies in the selected maximal ideal and whose leading coefficient
lies outside its contraction. Inverting that one coefficient makes the
polynomial monic. `isLocalization_adjoin_singleton` proves that localizing
the coefficients localizes the actual adjunction at their images; its proof
uses finite denominator clearing. The localization axioms are proved at
variable rings before specializing to these literal subalgebras, and a named
coefficient algebra is shared with the consumer. Localization also gives the needed Krull
dimension inequality by contraction of prime ideals.
