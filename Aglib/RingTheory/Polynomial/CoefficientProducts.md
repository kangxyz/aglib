# Polynomial coefficient products and local generators

[CoefficientProducts.lean](CoefficientProducts.lean) supplies the ring arguments
for extending a fixed projective coordinate row after blowing up a coefficient
ideal. All field-valued coefficients and ratios use the stated original maps.

## Nonzero contraction

For a nontrivial commutative ring R, an integral R-algebra S which is a domain,
and an injective original algebra map R→S,
Polynomial.exists_nonzero_map_eq_mul_of_integral takes p≠0 in S[Z] and returns
F≠0 in R[Z] and Q≠0 in S[Z] with

    map(R→S)(F) = p Q.

The coefficients of p make p integral over R[Z]. Nonzero contraction of the
principal ideal (p) gives F and the literal divisibility equation. Injectivity
of the polynomial coefficient map makes Q nonzero. No normality, locality,
finite-module basis, separability, or norm formula is required.

## Products in the same field

For a field K and a subring R integrally closed in K,
Subring.coeff_mul_coeff_mem_of_coeff_mul_mem proves

    all coefficients of p q lie in R
    ⇒ p.coeff i * q.coeff j lies in R for every i,j.

The hypotheses concern integrally closedness in that same K, not normality
in some smaller field. For each valuation subring V of K containing R,
normalize p and q by respective coefficients of maximal multiplicative
valuation. Each normalized polynomial has a coefficient equal to one.
The product therefore has unit coefficient ideal. A V-linear combination
of its coefficients equal to one shows that the product of the two selected
coefficients lies in V. Hence every cross-product lies in V. The intersection
of these valuation overrings is R, by [Stacks, Lemma 10.50.11,
Tag 090P](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/090P).

Polynomial.isIntegral_coeff_mul_coeff applies this argument to the actual
integral-closure subring of K. It concludes that all coefficient cross-products
are integral over the original base when the coefficients of the product
are integral. Polynomial.exists_algebraMap_eq_coeff_mul_coeff specializes
to a normal domain and its specified fraction field: it returns an original
base-ring representative of each cross-product.

The proof uses coefficient ideals, the pinned primitive-product theorem,
and valuation-overring separation. It does not use gcd-primitivity or a
Dedekind–Mertens identity.

## An original coefficient generates the local row

Polynomial.exists_coeff_generator_of_contentIdeal_eq_top takes a normal
local domain R with specified fraction field K, p,F in R[Z], q in K[Z],
the literal equation

    map(R→K)(F) = map(R→K)(p) q,

and F.contentIdeal=⊤. It returns an original coefficient p.coeff i≠0 such that
p.contentIdeal is the principal ideal generated by that coefficient.
For each original coefficient p.coeff k it also returns r in R with

    p.coeff k = p.coeff i * r,
    map(R→K)(r) = map(R→K)(p.coeff k) / map(R→K)(p.coeff i).

Every cross-product belongs to R by the preceding result. They cannot all
lie in the maximal ideal, since that would put every coefficient of F there.
Choose a unit cross-product. Multiplying other cross-products by its inverse
gives the displayed divisibility and ratio witnesses.

The selected coefficient need not be a unit of R. It is a frame of the
invertible ideal generated by the row; dividing the row by that frame gives
a projective coordinate equal to one. The result therefore provides the
local data needed by the finite-domination geometry without a supplied
principal-row or projective-extension premise.
