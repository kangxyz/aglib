# Original double-line charts and residue cubics

This module supplies the local algebra between the actual tangent-square
associated graded ring and the corrected Case-II point argument.

`exists_original_cubic_relation_nonzero` starts with the original Noetherian
local domain `A`, its three displayed maximal-ideal generators `t,s,z`, a
nonzero `t`, normality of the actual saturated `t`-chart, and a normalized
presentation of the actual associated graded ring as the double-line cone.
The presentation sends the original degree-one `t` and `z` classes to the
displayed cone coordinates. The theorem constructs an exact cubic relation
in `A` whose binary cubic, reduced through the original residue map, is
nonzero.

The proof first derives `z² ∈ m³` from the original associated graded
relation and writes the actual element using the ten cubic monomials in
`t,s,z`. It divides that true equation in the saturated chart. This is an
equation in the original ring; it is not asserted to present that ring.

The exceptional quotient is identified through the actual Rees quotient and
degree-one dehomogenization. Setting the distinguished cone parameter to
one produces the double-line quotient, with all original numerator
formulas retained. The prime `(t,z/t)` and its annihilator condition follow
from that quotient. The principal ideal theorem and normality give a DVR
at this actual prime. Total divisibility and cancellation force the cubic
coefficient to be a unit there. Locality of the original coefficient map
then proves nonvanishing of the same residue cubic, without a coefficient
field section or a numerical valuation choice.

At a singular dimension-two child with the actual three displayed
generators, the actual cubic coefficient must vanish in its residue field.
A separate polynomial lemma shows that a scalar square with degree at
most one in a distinguished variable has zero linear coefficient. These
are inputs to the remaining child tangent-coordinate argument; the module
does not infer that argument from a weaker tangent-rank condition.

For a nonzero polynomial of degree at most three, every repeated
irreducible divisor has degree one over the original field. A monic such
divisor is the actual polynomial `X - α`, and two repeated irreducibles
are associated. These statements require neither perfectness nor a
residue-characteristic assumption.

The actual RDP supplier must still provide the normalized associated
graded presentation and normality of its ordinary blowup charts. It must
also align the actual child tangent relation to prove the repeated-prime
square divisibility, cover both projective charts, and construct and
propagate the corrected simple-root marking. This module supplies no
marking, formal arc, completion property or desired termination as an
input, and does not conclude the full RDP or resolution theorem.
