# Reduced completions from associated DVR localizations

Import `Aglib.RingTheory.AdicCompletion.AssociatedPrimeCriterion`.

`AdicCompletion.isReduced_of_associated_dvr_quotients R x hx hx0`
applies to a Noetherian local domain R and a nonzero x in its maximal
ideal. For every actual associated prime p of R/(x), it requires:

- the ambient localization `Localization.AtPrime p` is a DVR;
- the actual maximal-ideal completion of R/p is reduced.

The conclusion is reducedness of the actual maximal-ideal completion
of R. The DVR and reduced-completion inputs remain explicit; they are
not inferred from a Nagata, excellence, or normalization oracle.
All rings and constructed carriers lie in the same ambient universe.

`IsRegularLocalRing.atPrime_of_flat_of_dvr_of_reduced_quotient` is the
local algebra used in the proof. For a flat R-algebra S with S Noetherian,
actual primes p and q with `p = q.comap (algebraMap R S)`, it assumes
that R_p is a domain DVR, S/pS is reduced, and q is associated to S/pS.
It concludes that the ambient S_q is regular. The original R need not
be Noetherian for this local statement.

The map R_p -> S_q is `Localization.localRingHom` for that precise
contraction equation. Localized flatness is derived from the original
flat algebra. The actual S_q/pS_q is a localization of S/pS, so it is
reduced. Associated-prime localization and
[`Ideal.eq_maximalIdeal_of_isReduced_quotient_of_isAssociatedPrime`](../Ideal/AssociatedPrime/Reduced.md)
identify pS_q with the maximal ideal of S_q. An actual DVR uniformizer
generates this ideal, and its image is a nonzerodivisor by flatness.
The quotient is consequently the residue field. The existing regular
principal-quotient theorem gives regularity of S_q. This is the
regularity conclusion needed from
[Stacks, Lemma 10.162.11 (032Z)](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/032Z),
with the original local map made explicit.

`IsReduced.of_flat_of_associated_dvr_quotients` treats a Noetherian flat
R-algebra S before specializing to completion. Here R is a Noetherian
domain, x is nonzero, and S is separated for the ideal generated by the
image of x. At every associated prime p of R/(x), it requires the same
ambient DVR condition and reduced S/pS. The original principal quotient
S/(image x) is compared with `S ⊗[R] (R ⧸ Ideal.span {x})` by the native
quotient-tensor equivalence. The
[flat associated-prime theorem](../Ideal/AssociatedPrime/FlatBaseChange.md)
supplies the actual contracted p for each associated q. The local result
above gives regularity of S_q. The separated associated-localization
criterion then proves S reduced.

`AdicCompletion.quotientEquivPrimeCompletion R p` constructs the ring
equivalence between the original quotient of completion(R) by p and
the actual maximal-ideal completion of R/p. It composes the original
quotient-tensor comparison with
[`tensorEquivExtendedCompletion`](FiniteAlgebra.lean), applied to the
actual finite algebra R/p. Surjectivity of the original quotient map
identifies the extended maximal ideal with the maximal ideal of R/p.
The theorem about reductions modulo powers of the completion ideal is
not substituted for this arbitrary prime-quotient comparison.

For the final completion criterion, Noetherianity is supplied by
`AdicCompletion.isNoetherianRing`, and flatness and locality use the
native completion instances. The original coefficient map sends x
into the completion's maximal ideal. Noetherian local separatedness
supplies the required Hausdorff property. The explicit prime-quotient
equivalence transfers the given reducedness assumptions, allowing the
flat-algebra criterion to apply.

The classical statement
[Stacks, Lemma 10.162.12 (0330)](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0330)
derives the associated DVR inputs from no embedded primes of R/(x),
the principal height theorem, and regularity of the ambient R_p.
This module does not encode that derivation. Normal-domain and rational-
adjunction applications must supply their actual DVR proofs. Regularity
of a localized principal quotient is never an assumed replacement.

The current consumer is the bounded Nagata analytic-unramifiedness
argument for surfaces. The finite semilocal normalization-completion
comparison and dimension induction remain separate. The F2/CN2 theorem
for nonnormal complete surfaces is still needed in the other direction,
from a reduced completion to finite normalization. No full Nagata-AU2,
arbitrary finite-type Nagata stability, or resolution theorem is claimed
by these interfaces.
